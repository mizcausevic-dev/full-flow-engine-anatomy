    A full-flow staged combustion engine turns each propellant into drive gas, takes shaft work out of that gas, and then burns the gas in the main chamber. The turbine exhaust is not dumped. That single choice is the cycle. The player above is the drawing. The rest of this page is how to read it, what it refuses to claim, and which public sources the cycle description actually rests on.

    Consolidated working brief, not a certified engineering reference. Verify hardware-specific details before you treat any sentence as an input to a design, a test, or a publication that states a number.

    What the cycle has to close

    A nozzle can only accelerate the gas it is given. Pumps, rather than tank pressure alone, are what put that gas at chamber pressure. A pump needs a turbine. A turbine needs drive gas. The design question is where the drive gas comes from, and where it goes after it has done the work.

    An open gas-generator cycle burns a little propellant to spin the turbines and throws that exhaust overboard. It never enters the main chamber. Staged combustion does not throw it away. A fuel-rich staged-combustion engine runs one preburner rich in fuel. An oxidizer-rich engine runs one preburner rich in oxygen. Full-flow runs both.

    All of the fuel and all of the oxidizer are pumped, and both pass through a preburner before the main chamber. The fuel turbopump is driven by fuel-rich gas. The oxidizer turbopump is driven by oxidizer-rich gas. Each turbine exhaust is still mostly the propellant that machine is responsible for, and that exhaust is injected into the chamber. The chamber is where the two streams finally meet in proportions that can finish the reaction.

    NASA’s 2006 account of the Integrated Powerhead Demonstrator describes that arrangement on a liquid-oxygen, liquid-hydrogen ground engine: a fuel-rich preburner drove the fuel turbopump, and an oxidizer-rich preburner drove the oxygen turbopump. The release’s reason for burning all of the propellant in the preburners is more mass flow through the turbines than in a conventional staged-combustion cycle, which is what lets the turbines run cooler. It also says that matching each turbine’s gas to its own propellant removes a class of interpropellant seal. The demonstrator was rated at about 250,000 pounds of thrust. It was not a flight engine, and it was not methalox. Those figures belong to that program, not to the plate on this page.

    SpaceX’s Raptor family is the methalox engine people mean when full-flow and flight show up in the same sentence. Methalox here means liquid methane and liquid oxygen. The Raptor article on Wikipedia describes an oxygen-rich turbine driving the oxygen pump, a fuel-rich turbine driving the methane pump, and both streams mixing in the gas phase before the chamber. It also describes Raptor as the first full-flow engine to fly. This page uses that description for the cycle class. It does not use it as a license to print chamber pressure, mixture ratio, or shaft speed.

    How to watch the 34 seconds

    The film is 1,020 frames at 30 frames per second, indexed from 0 through 1019. Same frame, same picture. The camera eases. It does not cut. Each shot still shows the hardware that caused it. Flow chevrons are time-compressed, and the plate says so. From frame 960 the camera is already still. By frame 975 the chevrons and the shafts have stopped. The last second and a half is a held view of the whole section.

    0:00–0:05 · Frames 0–149 · Feeds and pumps

    Liquid oxygen and liquid methane arrive on separate lines from the vehicle tanks. Each enters its own turbopump. The gold shafts already run down toward the turbines that drive them, so the cause of the pumping stays in frame. Impeller rotation marks shaft work. It is not a measured speed. A single coaxial stack, both pumps on one axis, is a different machine and is not drawn.

    0:05–0:10 · Frames 150–299 · Two preburners, two ratios

    Each pump discharge splits. The thick line is the majority of that propellant. The thin line is the minority sent across, because each preburner still needs a little of the other fluid in order to burn. Oxidizer-rich takes most of the oxygen and a little methane. Fuel-rich takes most of the methane and a little oxygen. Line weight is the only ratio on the plate. Inside each can, liquid becomes gas. Violet marks that change. The gas is there to drive a turbine, not to finish combustion. How far off ratio, and how hot, are the numbers this film refuses.

    0:10–0:17 · Frames 300–509 · Turbine gas to the chamber

    The gas leaves through its own turbine. Gold is the work sent back up the shaft to the pump. Cyan follows the gas inward and down. Both streams enter the main injector. In full-flow the turbine exhaust is still propellant. It is not dumped overboard. The pumps in the first shot are the machines this gas is driving. The camera travels with the gas so that link stays visible.

    0:17–0:25 · Frames 510–749 · Chamber and wall

    The two streams meet and burn continuously. The fill is a flame process, deflagration, not a detonation. There is no shock-cell “explosion” in the bore, because that picture teaches the wrong mechanism. Methane in the wall jacket picks up heat that would otherwise stay in the metal. Gold ticks are the energy crossing into the coolant. They move slower than the flow chevrons. The label calls that slow motion, and it calls the enlarged wall a change of scale.

    A transverse detail, the small row of passages, is a second view across the wall. It is not a claim about channel count. The downcomer that tees off the fuel line and runs to the jacket is a schematic tap. A flight engine may send all of the fuel through the jacket before the preburner. This drawing does not settle that branch. Public descriptions of Raptor, including a 2019 plume calculation that calls the booster nozzle regeneratively cooled, support the function. They do not, in this project, fix one plumbing diagram for every Raptor version.

    0:25–0:34 · Frames 750–1019 · Nozzle expansion

    The bore narrows to a throat and opens into a bell. That shape is a de Laval nozzle. At the design condition the flow is subsonic in the chamber, sonic at the throat, and supersonic in the bell. Violet names those states. The inset repeats them and prints no Mach number and no pressure. The arrows that grow toward the exit mean the gas is speeding up as it expands. They are not a survey. The chamber remains at the top of the frame while the bell comes in, which is the causal link: expansion is what the chamber gas does next. The camera then pulls back and holds.

    What the three colors are

    They are ink, not physics. Cyan is the primary pathway. Gold is energy or actuation: shaft work, and heat entering the coolant. Violet is a change of state: liquid to gas, separate streams to a reacting mixture, subsonic to sonic to supersonic. The metal stays a muted steel. Oxygen and methane inside a pipe have their own dull colors, so the fluids can be told apart with the accents ignored. If those accents were emission, the engine would be a light show. It is not.

    What was left out, and why

    • Chamber pressure, pump discharge pressure, and turbine temperature. A real value is a measured claim. This model does not earn one. The 250,000-pound figure above is NASA’s, for the hydrogen demonstrator, and it is fenced off from the plate.
    • Shaft speed. A real turbopump turns far faster than these impellers. A readable rotation and a printed RPM are two different acts. Only the first one happens.
    • Gimbal angle. Steering is a vehicle problem. It is not this cycle.
    • A coaxial pump. Wrong machine for the point this film is making.
    • Detonation. Wrong combustion mode for this chamber. A rotating detonation engine is a different subject.
    • A sensor with a number. Violet is state, not a transducer.
    • Injector element count, channel count, and the exact cooling tap. Drawn as schematic, labeled as schematic.
    • Thrust, specific impulse, throttle range, and any sentence that starts with “the most efficient.” Those need a test report.

    The film also compresses time. A slug of methane does not cross a real engine in a few seconds of screen time, and heat does not cross a liner on the beat of a chevron. The chips say so. Start, ignition, and shutdown are outside the timeline. This is steady operation.

    Where the complexity earns its keep

    Two preburners mean two hot-gas systems, two mixture ratios, and an oxidizer-rich turbine environment that is hard on metal. The reason to accept that, in the NASA account of the demonstrator and in the standard telling of the cycle, is mass flow. With all of the propellant available to the turbines, each turbine can produce the pump work at a lower temperature than a preburner that processes only a bleed. I am stating that motive, not a measured turbine temperature for any flying engine.

    The chamber then burns two streams that are already gas. That is a real architectural consequence for the main injector. It is not a claim about how any particular injector is manufactured. Compared with fuel-rich staged combustion, full-flow adds the oxidizer-rich preburner and sends both turbine streams to the chamber. Compared with an open gas-generator cycle, it refuses to dump the turbine exhaust. Those comparisons stand without a spec sheet.

    How the picture is built

    The picture is not a video file. It is a function of the frame index. Frame 600 is the same picture every time you land on it. Camera position is an eased interpolation between authored keys, with zero velocity at each key so the moves do not kick. There is no random particle system. Chevrons march by a fixed distance per frame, then ease to a stop for the held view. Parts do not fly apart. The cut is a section. When a mechanism could not be drawn without pretending, it became a labeled schematic instead of a fake part.

    That rule is the product. An anatomy that invents a chamber pressure in order to look finished is a less useful anatomy. The page is for an engineer, a technical writer, or a student who wants the cycle without a costume of precision. It does not collect a lead, sell a seat, or rank an engine.     Questions the plate is meant to answer

    What is full-flow staged combustion?

    Both propellants are burned in their own preburners. Each preburner drives its own turbine. Both turbine streams go into the main chamber and finish the reaction there. Nothing in the drive gas is thrown overboard.

    How is it different from a gas-generator cycle?

    A gas-generator cycle uses a small burner to spin the turbines and dumps that exhaust. Full-flow keeps the exhaust, because the exhaust is the propellant.

    Why two preburners instead of one?

    One stream has to stay oxidizer-rich to drive the oxygen pump. The other has to stay fuel-rich to drive the fuel pump. A single preburner cannot be both.

    Is this a Raptor drawing?

    It is a Raptor-inspired model of the cycle Raptor flies: methalox, full-flow, two turbopumps. It is not a manufacturing drawing, a measured section, or a statement of Raptor’s pressure, speed, or mixture ratio.

    Why is the combustion not called a detonation?

    Because it is not one. The chamber supports a continuous flame. Detonation is a shock-coupled mode. Drawing diamonds or a blast in the bore would teach the wrong mechanism.

    What do the colors mean?

    Cyan is the path. Gold is work or heat. Violet is a change of state. They are labels. The hardware is the steel.
